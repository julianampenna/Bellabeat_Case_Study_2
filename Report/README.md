## Well, this was quite the nightmare…

Dramatics aside, the process of cleaning the dataset and even grasping what this case study actually expected us to achieve was a much less straightforward journey than the Case Study 1 project from the Google Data Analytics Certificate.

For starters, although the orientation document provided a nice introduction to the context—even giving us a website for the company featured in the fictional scenario—it was, in my opinion, a bit vague when it came to clearly establishing the case study's purpose.

I'll try to walk you through my initial doubts—hopefully without making myself look too dumb in the process…

In the Scenario description (page 1), it says: “Urška Sršen (...) believes that analyzing smart device fitness data could help unlock new growth opportunities for the company. You have been asked to focus on one of Bellabeat’s products and analyze smart device data to gain insight into how consumers are using their smart devices.”

Up to this point, I was under the impression that the dataset (whichever it was and wherever it came from) was meant to be attributed to one or more of Bellabeat's smart devices, which the case study then goes on to present on page 2.

Following the list of products, the document proceeds with the company's history, stating that “collecting data on activity, sleep, stress, and reproductive health has allowed Bellabeat to empower women with knowledge about their own health and habits”... See where I'm going with this?

It is only on page 3 that the document mentions that the dataset at hand comes from a non-Bellabeat product. Okay, maybe all the initial fluff is meant to mimic real-life prolixity in the business world, but I'm guessing this must have been confusing not only for me, but also for many others who came to this study after completing Case Study 1—which had a much clearer purpose and, unlike this one, used a dataset that was meant to belong to the fictional company we were working for.

The vagueness in the wording of assessment questions, combined with a certain purism regarding terminology, was a recurring feature I noticed throughout the Google Data Analytics Certificate course. I get that terms and names are important. But mixing them up in poorly worded questions to set booby traps for course participants is not what I would consider an ideal way of testing one's knowledge of terminology. But I'm digressing…

Back to Case Study 2. My next challenge, after finally understanding that this was an analysis of external data rather than internal company data (yay me!), was to grasp how I was supposed to connect the business objective presented to us with the dataset we were expected to analyze.

To put it bluntly, I failed to see how the data could offer any insights that might point to an actionable business strategy for any of Bellabeat's products. How exactly was I supposed to find evidence that could tell Bellabeat how to market its products if the dataset contained behavioural information rather than marketing information?

So, after searching online and trying to find existing analyses that had been published—I barely found any, shocking—I realised that the purpose of the study was exactly that: establishing the missing connection, finding a bridge between behavioural data and marketing strategies.

However, even after reassuring myself that I had solved the great mystery of what Case Study 2 of the Google Data Analytics Certificate was actually asking us to do, the dataset itself presented additional hurdles.

Unnecessarily long tables that crashed my browser more than once, along with dates and times in incompatible formats, resulted in a very annoying cleaning process. This felt even more frustrating when, after cleaning eleven datasets, I eventually discovered that my first analytical question could be answered using just one of them.

That being said, I'm aware that I haven't yet completed the analysis, but I still can't help feeling a little tricked and deceived by the whole experience.

Well, Case Study 2, all I can say is that you handed my ass to me. This is what I've got for now, and I'm considering it a win. Good riddance!
